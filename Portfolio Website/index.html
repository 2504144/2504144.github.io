My CS30 major project · Spring 2025

Rubik's Cube simulator

A 3D Rubik's Cube you can scramble and then try to solve in your browser. You can spin the camera around and zoom in, change which keys turn which side, and pull up a notation guide from the sidebar if you forget what R or U' means.

What I learned: how to store a whole cube in a 3D array, so every turn moves the colours exactly like a real cube. The smooth turning animation was the hardest part. I got it partly working, but it kept fighting the cube logic, so I cut it to finish on time.

  • p5.js WEBGL
  • 3D arrays
  • HTML + CSS

CS30 grid game · 2025

Minesweeper

The classic, rebuilt from scratch. It has a start menu, a controls page, a shovel mode and a flag mode, and a bomb counter. Bombs get placed after your first click, so you never lose on turn one.

What I learned: playing video in p5.js with createVideo for the win screen, and using numFrames so the explosion only plays once instead of looping forever.

CS30 arrays project · March 2025

Maze generator

Every time you load it, a brand new random maze carves itself out right in front of you. A whistle tells you it's ready, then you steer a little player through with the keyboard, and a crowd cheers when you make it out.

What I learned: recursive backtracking, which is how the maze gets carved out, plus loading sounds with preload so they're ready the moment you need them.

  • p5.js
  • Recursion
  • Sound

CS30 interactive scene · February 2025

Two-player Pong

Full-screen Pong for two people on one keyboard (W/S versus the arrow keys), with a start menu and a controls page. My favourite part: scroll your mouse wheel mid-game and the ball speeds up or slows down.

What I learned: game states (menu, controls, playing), bouncing physics, and hooking up the mouse wheel as a control.

  • Game states
